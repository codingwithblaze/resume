# The PYQ Practice System {#pyq-system}

Previous-year questions are the most realistic practice available. This book doesn't reproduce them. It tells you **where to find them, in what order to use them, and how to extract the most from each one**.

## Where to get the papers

| Source | What you get | Trust level |
|---|---|---|
| **NTA** (jeemain.nta.nic.in) | Question papers with provisional answer keys during each session's challenge window, then final answer keys | Official. The final key decides correctness [NTA-WEB] |
| **PYQ archives** such as ExamSIDE and MathonGo | Shift-wise and chapter-wise PYQs with solutions | Third-party. Excellent for practice, but check disputed answers against the NTA final key {{tag:third}} [T-EXAMSIDE], [T-MATHONGO] |
| **This book** | Original questions modelled on the repeated structures (page [[pyq-structures]]) | Original practice, not PYQs |

## The three-phase plan {{tag:rec}}

:::flow
Phase 1 · Chapter-wise (while studying a chapter) | After each module, solve that chapter's 2024–2026 PYQs, timed at 2 minutes per question. Log every error in the Mistake Book.
Phase 2 · Mixed revision (after the syllabus is complete) | Solve PYQs unit-mixed: 30 questions from 6 chapters per session. Mixing forces you to choose the method, which is what the exam requires [LS-ROH].
Phase 3 · Full shifts as mocks (last 45–60 days) | Solve complete shifts in 3 hours at your exam time. **Keep the 6 most recent shifts untouched** until the final three weeks, so your last mocks are papers you haven't seen.
:::

:::important Older papers (2019–2023)
They are still useful for Physics and Maths practice, but skip questions on topics removed in 2024 (page [[pyq-trends]]). Don't count scores on old papers as mock scores: the pattern and syllabus have changed.
:::

## Getting full value from every PYQ

:::strategy The 4-question review (after each PYQ set)
1. **Did I recognise the structure within 30 seconds?** If not, which clue should have told me?
2. **Was my method the shortest one?** Compare it with the solution. If a shortcut exists, add it to your shortcut list.
3. **If I got it wrong, which mistake type was it?** Classify it with the 8 mistake types (page [[mistake-book]]).
4. **Would I get a variant right?** Change one number or condition and re-solve it in your head.
:::

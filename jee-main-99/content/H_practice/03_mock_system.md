# The Mock-Test System {#mock-system}

A mock test has two jobs: to **rehearse the exam** and to **show you what to fix next**. Most students do the first and skip the second, which wastes most of the mock's value {{tag:rec}}.

## How many mocks, and when {{tag:rec}}

| Phase | When | Mocks | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · Building | while the syllabus is still in progress | 1 every 2 weeks | part-syllabus tests on completed chapters |
| B · Full syllabus | from the end of the syllabus to 45 days before the exam | 1 per week | full-length (original mocks, older PYQ shifts) |
| C · Intensive | last 45 to 10 days | 2–3 per week | full-length, including recent PYQ shifts |
| D · Final | last 10 days | 1 every 2–3 days, the last one 2–3 days before the exam | recent unseen shifts, at your exam time |
| Exam eve | — | **none** | formula sheets and rest only |

:::important The 1 : 1 rule
Spend at least as long analysing a mock as taking it (3 hours), within 24 hours. If a week has no time for analysis, take fewer mocks, not less analysis.
:::

## Mock conditions (non-negotiable)

:::grid2
**Replicate the exam**

- [ ] Same start time as your shift (9:00 or 15:00)
- [ ] 3 hours, no breaks, no phone in the room
- [ ] Only blank rough sheets and a pen
- [ ] On-screen CBT format whenever possible, with the NTA-style palette (answered / not answered / marked)
- [ ] Water allowed; no food during the paper
+++
**Replicate the plan**

- [ ] Follow your subject order and round plan (page [[hall-protocol]])
- [ ] Note the time at each checkpoint (e.g. 0:55, 1:55, 2:40)
- [ ] Write a code next to each skipped question: **S** (skip for now), **N** (never)
- [ ] Stop exactly at 3:00, then fill Part 1 of the analysis sheet **before** checking answers
:::

## The six-step analysis

:::flow
1 · Before checking answers | Record time per subject, the number attempted, and your confidence in each answer (sure / 50-50 / guess). This baseline shows whether your confidence is well calibrated.
2 · Score & breakdown | Marks per subject; correct, wrong and unattempted counts; accuracy = correct ÷ attempted.
3 · Question-by-question | Give every question a status: **C** correct · **W** wrong · **UK** unattempted but you *knew* how · **UN** unattempted and not known. UK questions are free marks lost.
4 · Classify every W and UK | Assign one of the 8 mistake types (page [[mistake-book]]) and write one line on what happened.
5 · Time audit | Which questions took more than 3 minutes? Did easy questions get skipped because time ran out?
6 · Three action items | No more than three, each specific ("re-derive rolling on an incline and solve 10 questions", not "revise rotation"). Put them into next week's plan.
:::

:::strategy The re-attempt
Seven days after a mock, re-attempt every W and UK question without looking at the solution. If you still get it wrong, it goes into the Mistake Book as a **repeat**, the highest-priority category.
:::

## Reading your mock trend

:::strategy What the numbers mean {{tag:rec}}
- **Accuracy below 80%** means slow down. You are over-attempting. Cut the attempts from the questions you are least sure of.
- **Accuracy above 92% with few attempts** means you are too cautious, or too slow. Work on speed in Round 1.
- **Many UK questions** is a time-management problem, not a knowledge problem. Fix selection and the 3-minute rule.
- **One subject much lower** decides the next two weeks of your plan. See the scenario tables (page [[percentile-strategy]]).
- **A single bad mock means nothing.** Judge the average of the last three.
:::

# Physics: The Complete Strategy {#physics-strategy}

## What Physics in JEE Main actually tests

JEE Main Physics is mostly **"one idea + clean calculation"**. A typical question identifies one principle (conservation of energy, Gauss's law, lens formula, Bohr's model), sets up a standard situation, and checks whether you can apply it quickly and without sign or unit errors. Long multi-concept problems do appear, but a minority of each paper is truly hard. The rest rewards **formula fluency, standard-model recognition and calculation discipline**. {{tag:third}} [T-ANALYSIS26]

:::fact Syllabus structure (official)
The official Physics syllabus has **20 units**. Units 1–19 are theory. **Unit 20, Experimental Skills,** lists 18 standard experiments (Vernier callipers, screw gauge, metre bridge, resonance tube, and others). The syllabus document states that the theory part carries 80% weightage and the experimental part 20% [NTA-SYL]. In practice, directly lab-based questions have been fewer than this per shift, but the experiments' underlying physics (errors, least count, graphs) is tested often. {{tag:third}}
:::

## How to study a Physics chapter (the 5-pass method) {{tag:rec}}

1. **Pass 1 · Understand (NCERT + module core theory).** Read NCERT for the chapter, then this module's *Concept summary*. Write the formula box by hand once.
2. **Pass 2 · Model bank.** List the chapter's standard models (block on incline, rolling body, charged sphere, LCR circuit). For each, write *the one equation you start from*.
3. **Pass 3 · Practice.** Do the module's practice set timed, then 30–60 questions from a standard question bank, then PYQs.
4. **Pass 4 · Error harvest.** Every wrong question goes into the Mistake Book with its type.
5. **Pass 5 · Compress.** Reduce the chapter to its half-page Formula Handbook entry and its trap list. Revise from those only.

## Using NCERT for Physics

NCERT Physics isn't sufficient on its own for JEE-level problem solving, but it's **necessary** for four things:

- **Definitions and statements** (Lenz's law, Huygens' principle, Brewster's law, Kepler's laws): statement-type questions come from here.
- **Semiconductors, EM waves, Dual Nature, Atoms, Nuclei**: NCERT depth is close to JEE Main depth in these chapters.
- **Units & Measurements** (significant figures, error rules) and the **Lab Manual**, which covers the Unit 20 experiments.
- **Graphs and tables** printed in NCERT (binding-energy curve, stress–strain curve, I–V characteristics, EM spectrum table).

## Physics: how to maximise marks

:::grid3
**Strong student (Physics ≥ 75/100 in mocks)**

- Aim for 22–23 attempts at 92%+ accuracy.
- Your losses are silly: signs, unit conversions, reading "radius" as "diameter". Keep a 10-item personal trap list and read it before every mock.
- Practise hard Rotation, EMI and Electrostatics *mixed* problems. These separate 90 from 75.
- Do Section B (NV) Physics first. NV questions are often direct, and strong students can bank them.
+++
**Average student (50–75)**

- Master the easy block first (above): it's worth 20–30 marks.
- For every medium chapter, learn the **three most-asked models** only. Don't collect tricks.
- In the exam, do Round 1 on "formula → numbers" questions only. Leave all multi-concept questions for Round 2.
- Weekly target: 150 Physics questions, with 40 of them PYQs.
+++
**Weak student (< 50)**

- Physics is where the formula handbook pays off most. Daily 20-minute blank-sheet formula recall.
- Chapter order: Units → Modern (17, 18, 19) → EM Waves → KTG → Current → Electrostatics → Optics → Thermodynamics → Gravitation, then Mechanics.
- Attempt 15–17 questions, all Easy/Medium, at 90%+ accuracy. That alone is 55–65 marks.
- Use dimensional analysis and limiting cases (Speed Manual) to verify answers.
:::

## Physics exam-hall rules {{tag:rec}}

- **Read the last line first.** Is it asking for speed or velocity, total or net, radius or diameter, in cm or m?
- **Write the "given" in SI units** before any algebra.
- **Estimate before you compute.** A rough order of magnitude eliminates 1–2 options in most numerical MCQs.
- **Two-minute rule.** No progress in 2 minutes means mark for review and move on.
- **Section B:** recompute once, using a different path where possible (energy vs force, for example), before entering the number.

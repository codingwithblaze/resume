# Mathematics: The Complete Strategy {#maths-strategy}

## What Mathematics in JEE Main actually tests

In 2026 analyses, Mathematics was repeatedly described as the **lengthiest section and the rank decider**, with multi-step calculations and several concepts combined in single questions. Coaching commentary suggested that students who spent much more than 60–70 minutes on Maths often struggled overall {{tag:third}} [T-ANALYSIS26]. The implication is clear: in Maths, **selection and calculation fitness** matter as much as knowing the chapters.

:::fact Syllabus structure (official)
The official Mathematics syllabus has **14 units** [NTA-SYL]. The 2024 revision removed Mathematical Induction and Mathematical Reasoning entirely, and trimmed topics inside units. For example, the 3D Geometry unit text now reads "…Equations of a line", with planes no longer listed; the vector unit lists scalar and vector products (triple products are not listed); Binomial lists "general term and middle term"; Sequences lists AP, GP and AM–GM. Each module below flags what is and isn't listed.
:::

:::warning "Removed" doesn't always mean "never asked"
Third-party PYQ archives list post-2024 3D questions that involve **planes** {{tag:third}} [T-EXAMSIDE]. This book's recommendation {{tag:rec}}: learn the **basic plane formulas** (normal form, distance of a point from a plane, angle between a line and a plane) as a cheap 2-hour insurance topic, and don't go deeper than that.
:::

:::important The "Must-do models" (fastest marks in Maths)
Vectors & 3D (dot/cross product, line equations, shortest distance) · Matrices & determinants (adjoint, inverse, systems of equations, properties) · Binomial general term · AP/GP sums · P&C arrangements · Definite-integral properties (King's rule, even/odd) · Linear differential equations · Probability with Bayes' theorem · Standard conics (focus, directrix, latus rectum, eccentricity) · Complex modulus/argument/locus. **These typically give 12–15 questions a shift** (third-party consensus of unit ranges) and are the backbone of the Weak-Maths strategy (page [[percentile-strategy]]).
:::

## How to study a Maths chapter {{tag:rec}}

1. **Formula sheet first.** Write the chapter's formulas and standard results (from this module) in your own notebook.
2. **Solve the solved examples** in NCERT, then this module's practice set with **full working**.
3. **Model drills:** for each "question model" in the module, solve 8–10 questions in a row. Repetition builds recognition.
4. **Timed PYQs:** chapter-wise PYQs from 2024–2026 at 2.5 minutes per question.
5. **Calculation fitness:** 15 minutes a day of hand arithmetic and algebra (Speed Manual, page [[speed-manual]]).
6. **Mixed practice** once a chapter is done: interleave it with earlier chapters. Mixing problem types improves the ability to *choose* a method [LS-ROH].

## Mathematics: how to maximise marks

:::grid3
**Strong student (≥ 75)**

- Attempt 20–22, but go through the paper **twice**: Round 1 takes only questions whose method you see in 20 seconds.
- Most of your lost marks are calculation errors. Use a verification habit on every NV answer (substitute back, check the limiting case).
- Build speed in integration and coordinate geometry. They carry the most marks and are the most lengthy.
+++
**Average student (45–75)**

- Master the Must-do models above before anything else.
- Target 16–18 attempts at 85%+ accuracy.
- The main loss is spending 8 minutes on one question. Enforce the **3-minute rule** in every mock.
- Learn standard results (area formulas, the $\int_0^{\pi/2}$ results, P&C formulas) cold.
+++
**Weak student (< 45)**

- Work on the Must-do models only for the first 4–6 weeks.
- Attempt 12–14 questions, all from the models, at 90% accuracy: about 45–50 marks.
- Daily: 20 easy questions from two models, plus 15 minutes of arithmetic drill. See also "Fear of Mathematics" (page [[psychology]]).
:::

## Maths exam-hall rules {{tag:rec}}

- **Read all 25 questions' first lines within 4 minutes** of starting Maths and tick those you recognise instantly.
- **Section B (NV) Maths** often includes direct questions (a determinant value, a coefficient, a count). Scan for them early.
- **Never do algebra in your head.** Write intermediate steps on the rough sheet. Most Maths errors are transcription slips.
- **Use the options:** substitution, special values, limiting cases and dimension/degree checks eliminate options fast (Answer-Choice Strategy, page [[answer-choice]]).
- **Stop rule:** if a question needs a second approach after 3 minutes, mark it and leave it.

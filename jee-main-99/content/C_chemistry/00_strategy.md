# Chemistry: The Complete Strategy {#chem-strategy}

## Why Chemistry decides 99 percentile

Chemistry is usually the **fastest and most accurate** section for well-prepared students. In 2026 analyses it was repeatedly described as the section where NCERT-strong students gained their edge, with several questions taken almost directly from NCERT text and lab manuals {{tag:third}} [T-ANALYSIS26]. A realistic 99+ plan treats Chemistry as the **mark bank**: 80–90/100 in about 45 minutes, leaving more time for Mathematics.

:::fact Syllabus structure (official)
The official Chemistry syllabus has **20 units**: 8 Physical (Units 1–8), 4 Inorganic (Units 9–12) and 8 Organic (Units 13–20, including Unit 20, Principles Related to Practical Chemistry). The 2024 revision removed States of Matter, Surface Chemistry, Hydrogen, s-Block, Metallurgy, Environmental Chemistry, Polymers and Chemistry in Everyday Life [NTA-SYL], [T-DELETED].
:::

## How to maximise marks in Chemistry

:::grid3
**Strong student (≥ 80)**

- Target 23–24 attempts at 95% accuracy in ≤ 40 minutes.
- Remaining losses usually come from NCERT exceptions and unusual organic conversions. Keep an **exceptions notebook** and read it weekly.
- Solve 2024–2026 PYQs **line by line against NCERT**, and highlight the NCERT sentence each one came from.
+++
**Average student (55–80)**

- Physical Chemistry formulas + Coordination + Biomolecules + Practical Chemistry = the quickest 20 marks to add.
- Organic: learn the reaction maps (not isolated reactions) for Units 15–18.
- Inorganic: one NCERT reading **every 10 days** for Units 9–12 (spaced repetition).
+++
**Weak student (< 55)**

- Start with **NCERT reading**, not question banks. Chemistry is where reading converts to marks most directly.
- Order: Biomolecules → Practical Chemistry → Coordination → Periodicity → Atomic Structure → Mole concept → Kinetics → Solutions → Electrochemistry → GOC → carbonyl compounds.
- Attempt only questions you recognise within 20 seconds. Chemistry questions reward recall, and hesitation usually signals a gap.
:::

# The NCERT Strategy {#ncert-strategy}

## Why NCERT, and how exactly

:::fact Alignment
The 2024 syllabus revision aligned JEE Main with the **rationalised NCERT** textbooks [NTA-SYL]. Inorganic Chemistry, Biomolecules and Practical Chemistry questions are frequently answerable from NCERT text, tables and in-text examples {{tag:third}} [T-ANALYSIS26].
:::

**The 4-read method** {{tag:rec}}

1. **Read 1 · Understand** (with the module): read the whole chapter, including boxes, tables and in-text questions.
2. **Read 2 · Mark** (Day 7): highlight every fact that could be an MCQ: exceptions, colours, shapes, orders, named reactions, uses.
3. **Read 3 · Extract** (Day 14): copy the highlights into this book's NCERT checklists or your own fact sheet.
4. **Read 4+ · Recall** (every 10–14 days): cover the page and recall the table, then check. Only the fact sheet is re-read after this point.

## Important factual areas (what to extract from NCERT)

:::grid2
**Tables & trends to memorise**

- Periodic trends: atomic/ionic radii, IE, EGE, EN, with every exception
- Shapes & hybridisation (VSEPR table)
- MO diagrams for $\ce{B2}$ to $\ce{Ne2}$: bond order, magnetism
- d-block: electronic configurations (Cr, Cu anomalies), oxidation states, colours, magnetic moments
- Lanthanoid contraction and its consequences
- Spectrochemical series; crystal field splitting ($\Delta_o$ vs pairing)
- Acid/base strengths: carboxylic acids, phenols, amines (gas vs water)
- Vitamins and deficiency diseases; bases in DNA vs RNA
+++
**Reactions, exceptions & lines**

- Preparation of $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ and $\ce{KMnO4}$ (steps and equations)
- All named reactions in Class 12 organic chapters (list on page [[c17]])
- Tests: Lucas, iodoform, Tollens', Fehling's, Hinsberg, carbylamine, FeCl₃ (phenol)
- Reimer–Tiemann, Kolbe, Cannizzaro, aldol, Gabriel phthalimide, Hofmann bromamide, Sandmeyer, diazo coupling
- Glucose: reactions showing its structure (HI, $\ce{NH2OH}$, Br₂ water, $\ce{HNO3}$, acetylation)
- Anomalous behaviour of the first element of each p-block group
- Uses of coordination compounds (EDTA, cisplatin, Wilkinson's catalyst, estimation of hardness)
- Every "Why?" statement in the in-text questions
:::

## Chemistry revision method

1. **Physical:** formula sheet + 10 numerical questions per chapter per week. Formula recall on a blank sheet every 5 days.
2. **Inorganic:** fact sheets read on a **spaced schedule** (Day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30). 15-minute "flash review" every night in the final 45 days.
3. **Organic:** redraw each **reaction map** from memory once a week; solve 20 conversion questions per week.
4. **Mistake Book:** every Chemistry error tagged *fact / concept / exception / calculation*. Exceptions become flash cards.

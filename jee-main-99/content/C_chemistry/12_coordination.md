## Important concepts

- **Werner's theory:** metals have **primary valency** (ionisable, equal to the oxidation state, satisfied by anions) and **secondary valency** (non-ionisable, equal to the coordination number, directed in space).
- **Ligands:** unidentate ($\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{H2O}$), bidentate (en, $\ce{C2O4^{2-}}$), polydentate ($\ce{EDTA^{4-}}$, hexadentate). **Ambidentate** ligands bind through two different atoms: $\ce{NO2-}$/$\ce{ONO-}$, $\ce{SCN-}$/$\ce{NCS-}$, $\ce{CN-}$/$\ce{NC-}$. **Chelates** (ring-forming) are extra stable (the chelate effect).
- **Homoleptic** complexes have one kind of ligand ($\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^{3+}}$); **heteroleptic** complexes have more than one ($\ce{[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+}$).
- **Bonding:** VBT gives hybridisation and geometry. **CFT** explains colour, magnetism and the spectrochemical series.

## IUPAC nomenclature: the rules

1. Name the **cation first**, then the anion (as for any salt).
2. Inside the complex: **ligands in alphabetical order** (ignoring multiplying prefixes), then the metal.
3. Anionic ligands end in **-o**: chlorido, cyanido, hydroxido, oxido, oxalato, nitrito-N (NO₂), nitrito-O (ONO), thiocyanato-S/-N. Neutral: **aqua, ammine, carbonyl, nitrosyl**, en (ethane-1,2-diamine).
4. Prefixes di-, tri-, tetra-; for ligands whose names already contain a number, bis-, tris-, tetrakis-.
5. Oxidation state of the metal in Roman numerals. In an **anionic complex** the metal name ends in **-ate**: ferrate (Fe), cuprate (Cu), argentate (Ag), aurate (Au), plumbate (Pb), stannate (Sn), cobaltate, nickelate, zincate.

| $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2}$ | pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride |
| $\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$ | potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III) |

## Isomerism

:::formula Types
**Structural:** ionisation ($\ce{[Co(NH3)5SO4]Br}$ / $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$), linkage ($\ce{-NO2}$ / $\ce{-ONO}$), coordination ($\ce{[Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6]}$ / $\ce{[Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]}$), solvate/hydrate ($\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]Cl3}$ violet / $\ce{[Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2.H2O}$ grey-green / $\ce{[Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl.2H2O}$ dark green).
**Stereo:** geometrical (cis/trans, fac/mer) and optical (non-superimposable mirror images).

| Complex type | Geometrical isomers | Optical? |
|---|---|---|
| square planar $\ce{MA2B2}$ | 2 (cis, trans) | no |
| square planar $\ce{MABCD}$ | 3 | no |
| tetrahedral (any) | 0 | only if 4 different groups (rare) |
| octahedral $\ce{MA4B2}$ | 2 (cis, trans) | no |
| octahedral $\ce{MA3B3}$ | 2 (fac, mer) | no |
| octahedral $\ce{M(AA)2B2}$ | 2 | **cis form is chiral** (1 pair) |
| octahedral $\ce{M(AA)3}$ | — | **yes** (Δ and Λ) |
| octahedral $\ce{MA2B2C2}$ | 5 | one of them is chiral (6 stereoisomers in total) |
:::

## Crystal field theory

:::formula Splitting & CFSE
**Octahedral:** $t_{2g}$ (3 orbitals, $-0.4\Delta_o$ each electron) below $e_g$ (2 orbitals, $+0.6\Delta_o$).
**Tetrahedral:** reversed ($e$ below $t_2$), $\Delta_t = \tfrac49\Delta_o$, so tetrahedral complexes are almost always **high spin**.
$$\text{CFSE} = (-0.4\,n_{t_{2g}} + 0.6\,n_{e_g})\Delta_o\ (+\ \text{pairing energy terms})$$
If $\Delta_o > P$ (pairing energy), electrons pair up in $t_{2g}$ (low spin). If $\Delta_o < P$, they occupy $e_g$ first (high spin).
**Spectrochemical series (weak → strong field):**
$\ce{I-} < \ce{Br-} < \ce{SCN-} < \ce{Cl-} < \ce{S^{2-}} < \ce{F-} < \ce{OH-} < \ce{C2O4^{2-}} < \ce{H2O} < \ce{NCS-} < \text{edta}^{4-} < \ce{NH3} < \text{en} < \ce{CN-} < \ce{CO}$
:::

:::important Colour
The colour seen is **complementary** to the light absorbed in the d–d transition. $\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]^{3+}}$ ($t_{2g}^1$) absorbs in the blue-green region (~498 nm) and looks violet. Anhydrous $\ce{CuSO4}$ is white; $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$ is blue (water ligands produce the splitting). Stronger-field ligands give a larger $\Delta_o$, so absorption shifts to shorter wavelength.
:::

:::important Metal carbonyls
Bonding is **synergic**: CO donates a lone pair to the metal (σ), and the metal back-donates d electrons into CO's π* orbital (π back-bonding). Result: the M–C bond gains double-bond character, while the C–O bond weakens and lengthens. Examples: $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ (tetrahedral), $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ (trigonal bipyramidal), $\ce{[Cr(CO)6]}$ (octahedral). The metal is in oxidation state 0.
:::

## Importance of coordination compounds (NCERT list)

| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| Qualitative analysis | $\ce{Ni^{2+}}$ + dimethylglyoxime → red precipitate; Cu²⁺ + NH₃ → deep blue $\ce{[Cu(NH3)4]^{2+}}$ |
| Hardness of water | EDTA titration of $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$/$\ce{Mg^{2+}}$ |
| Metallurgy | extraction of Ag and Au as cyanido complexes, e.g. $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$; purification of Ni via $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ |
| Biology | chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe), vitamin B₁₂ (Co) |
| Medicine | **cisplatin** (anticancer); EDTA for **lead poisoning** |
| Catalysis | **Wilkinson's catalyst** $\ce{[(Ph3P)3RhCl]}$ for hydrogenation of alkenes |
| Photography | $\ce{[Ag(S2O3)2]^{3-}}$ formed when fixing with hypo |

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Oxidation state vs coordination number: in $\ce{[Co(en)3]^{3+}}$ the CN is **6** (3 bidentate ligands), not 3.
- Only ions **outside** the square brackets precipitate with $\ce{AgNO3}$.
- A strong-field ligand does **not** always mean inner-orbital hybridisation. Check the d count: $\ce{[Ni(NH3)6]^{2+}}$ ($d^8$) is sp³d² whatever the ligand.
- Tetrahedral complexes: essentially always high spin, and no geometrical isomers.
- Alphabetical order of ligands ignores prefixes: "tetraammine" is ordered under **a**.
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. IUPAC names ↔ formulae (including the -ate endings).
2. Isomer counts (geometrical/optical) for octahedral and square-planar complexes.
3. Hybridisation, geometry, magnetic moment (VBT) of standard complexes.
4. CFT: CFSE, high/low spin, colour, spectrochemical series ordering.
5. Werner-type questions (moles of AgCl precipitated, conductivity/number of ions).

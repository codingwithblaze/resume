## Part A · Alcohols

:::formula Preparation
- **Alkenes:** acid-catalysed hydration (Markovnikov); **hydroboration–oxidation** ($\ce{B2H6}$, then $\ce{H2O2}$/$\ce{OH-}$) gives **anti-Markovnikov** alcohols (propene → propan-1-ol).
- **Reduction of carbonyls:** $\ce{H2}$/Pd, $\ce{NaBH4}$ or $\ce{LiAlH4}$. Aldehydes → 1°, ketones → 2°. Acids and esters need $\ce{LiAlH4}$ (→ 1°).
- **Grignard:** HCHO → 1°; other aldehydes → 2°; ketones → 3° (after hydrolysis).
:::

:::rmap Reaction map · ethanol $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$
Na | $\ce{CH3CH2ONa + 1/2 H2}$ (acidic character)
RCOOH, conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ (Fischer) | ester $\ce{RCOOC2H5}$
HCl/$\ce{ZnCl2}$ · $\ce{PCl5}$ · $\ce{SOCl2}$ | $\ce{CH3CH2Cl}$
conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 443 K | ethene (dehydration)
conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 413 K (excess alcohol) | diethyl ether
PCC | ethanal (stops at aldehyde)
acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ / $\ce{KMnO4}$ | ethanoic acid
Cu, 573 K | ethanal (dehydrogenation)
$\ce{I2}$/NaOH | iodoform (yellow): ethanol has the $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ unit
:::

:::important Dehydration mechanism (syllabus-listed)
(1) Protonation of –OH → $\ce{R-OH2+}$; (2) loss of $\ce{H2O}$ gives a **carbocation** (slow step); (3) loss of β-H gives the alkene (Saytzeff). Ease: **3° > 2° > 1°**, following carbocation stability. Rearrangements are possible.
:::

:::formula Distinguishing 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols
| Test | 1° | 2° | 3° |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lucas** (conc. HCl + anhydrous $\ce{ZnCl2}$) | no turbidity at room temperature | turbidity in ~5 min | **immediate** turbidity |
| Oxidation ($\ce{K2Cr2O7}$/$\ce{H+}$) | aldehyde → acid | ketone | resistant |
| Cu at 573 K | aldehyde | ketone | alkene |

Acidity (in solution): 1° > 2° > 3°. All are weaker acids than water, and much weaker than phenol.
:::

## Part B · Phenols

:::formula Preparation & acidity
**Preparation:** chlorobenzene (Dow: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm); benzenesulphonic acid (fuse with NaOH, then acidify); **diazonium salt + warm water**; **cumene process** (cumene + $\ce{O2}$ → cumene hydroperoxide → dil. acid → phenol + **acetone**).
**Acidity:** phenoxide is resonance-stabilised, so phenol (p$K_a$ 10.0) is far more acidic than ethanol (15.9). It reacts with NaOH, but **not with $\ce{NaHCO3}$** (picric acid is the exception).
| Phenol | p$K_a$ (NCERT) |
|---|---|
| *p*-nitrophenol | 7.1 |
| *o*-nitrophenol | 7.2 |
| *m*-nitrophenol | 8.3 |
| phenol | 10.0 |
| cresols (o, m, p) | ~10.1–10.2 |
| 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) | ~0.4 (strongly acidic) |

Electron-withdrawing groups at **ortho/para** raise acidity (−R and −I). Electron-donating groups lower it.
:::

:::rmap Reaction map · phenol $\ce{C6H5OH}$
dil. $\ce{HNO3}$, 298 K | *o*- + *p*-nitrophenol (separated by steam distillation: *o* is volatile)
conc. $\ce{HNO3}$ | 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid)
$\ce{Br2}$ in $\ce{CS2}$, 273 K | *o*- + *p*-bromophenol
**bromine water** | 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate)
**Kolbe:** NaOH, then $\ce{CO2}$ (400 K, 4–7 atm), then $\ce{H+}$ | salicylic acid (*o*-hydroxybenzoic acid)
**Reimer–Tiemann:** $\ce{CHCl3}$ + aq. NaOH, then $\ce{H+}$ | salicylaldehyde (via dichlorocarbene $\ce{:CCl2}$)
Zn dust, heat | benzene
$\ce{Na2Cr2O7}$/$\ce{H2SO4}$ | benzoquinone
neutral $\ce{FeCl3}$ | violet colour (test)
:::

## Part C · Ethers

:::formula Preparation & reactions
**Dehydration of alcohols** (conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 413 K): works for 1° alcohols only (2°/3° give alkenes).
**Williamson synthesis:** $\ce{R-X + R'-ONa -> R-O-R' + NaX}$ ($\ce{S_N2}$). Use a **1° alkyl halide** and the bulky alkoxide: *tert*-butyl methyl ether = $\ce{(CH3)3CONa + CH3Br}$. The reverse combination ($\ce{(CH3)3CBr + CH3ONa}$) gives isobutylene by elimination.
**Structure:** C–O–C angle ≈ 111.7° (slightly above tetrahedral; bulky groups repel).
**Cleavage by HI:** $\ce{R-O-R' + HI -> R-I + R'-OH}$. With a methyl alkyl ether (1°), $\ce{I-}$ attacks the **methyl** carbon ($\ce{S_N2}$), giving $\ce{CH3I}$ + ROH. With a *tert*-alkyl group, the tertiary halide forms ($\ce{S_N1}$). **Anisole + HI → phenol + $\ce{CH3I}$**: the O–aryl bond doesn't break.
**Anisole EAS:** the $\ce{-OCH3}$ group is activating and o/p-directing (halogenation, nitration, Friedel–Crafts).
:::

## Part D · Aldehydes & Ketones

:::formula Preparation (named)
| Reaction | Transformation |
|---|---|
| **Rosenmund reduction** | $\ce{RCOCl + H2 ->[Pd/BaSO4] RCHO}$ |
| **Stephen reduction** | $\ce{RCN ->[SnCl2/HCl] RCH=NH ->[H3O+] RCHO}$ |
| DIBAL-H | nitriles/esters → aldehydes |
| **Etard reaction** | toluene + $\ce{CrO2Cl2}$ (chromyl chloride), then $\ce{H3O+}$ → benzaldehyde |
| Side-chain chlorination | toluene → $\ce{C6H5CHCl2}$ → hydrolysis → benzaldehyde |
| **Gattermann–Koch** | benzene + CO + HCl, anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$/CuCl → benzaldehyde |
| Ketones from nitriles | $\ce{RCN + R'MgX}$, then $\ce{H3O+}$ → $\ce{RCOR'}$ |
| Ketones from acyl chlorides | $\ce{2RCOCl + R'2Cd -> 2RCOR'}$ |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | benzene + RCOCl/$\ce{AlCl3}$ → aryl ketone |
| Ozonolysis; hydration of alkynes; oxidation of alcohols | see Units 15 and 17A |
:::

:::important Reactivity of the carbonyl group
The carbonyl carbon is sp², planar and electrophilic. **Nucleophilic addition** reactivity: HCHO > $\ce{CH3CHO}$ > $\ce{CH3COCH3}$. Alkyl groups donate electrons (+I) and add steric bulk. Aromatic carbonyls are less reactive (resonance with the ring).
:::

:::rmap Reaction map · ethanal $\ce{CH3CHO}$ (and ketones)
HCN (base) | cyanohydrin $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CN}$
$\ce{NaHSO3}$ | bisulphite adduct (crystalline; used for separation)
$\ce{R'MgX}$, then $\ce{H3O+}$ | 2° alcohol (ketones → 3°)
ROH, dry HCl | hemiacetal → acetal (ketones + ethylene glycol → cyclic ketal)
$\ce{NH2OH}$ | oxime
$\ce{NH2NH2}$ | hydrazone
2,4-DNP (Brady's reagent) | orange/yellow 2,4-DNP derivative (test for C=O)
semicarbazide | semicarbazone
$\ce{NaBH4}$ / $\ce{LiAlH4}$ | ethanol
**Clemmensen** (Zn–Hg / conc. HCl) | ethane ($\ce{>C=O -> >CH2}$)
**Wolff–Kishner** ($\ce{NH2NH2}$, then KOH/ethylene glycol, heat) | ethane
**Tollens'** reagent | silver mirror (aldehydes only)
**Fehling's** solution | red $\ce{Cu2O}$ (aliphatic aldehydes only)
$\ce{I2}$/NaOH (haloform) | iodoform + formate (all methyl carbonyls)
dil. NaOH (**aldol**) | 3-hydroxybutanal → (heat) but-2-enal
:::

:::formula Condensation & disproportionation
**Aldol:** aldehydes/ketones **with α-H** + dilute alkali → β-hydroxy carbonyl (aldol/ketol) → on heating, α,β-unsaturated carbonyl. **Cross-aldol** between two different carbonyls gives 4 products (fewer if one partner lacks α-H).
**Cannizzaro:** aldehydes **without α-H** (HCHO, $\ce{C6H5CHO}$, $\ce{(CH3)3CCHO}$) + **conc.** NaOH → alcohol + carboxylate salt (one molecule reduced, one oxidised). Cross-Cannizzaro with HCHO: HCHO is oxidised to formate.
**Haloform:** compounds with $\ce{CH3CO-}$ or $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ (ethanal, methyl ketones, ethanol, propan-2-ol) + $\ce{X2}$/NaOH → $\ce{CHX3}$. Iodoform is a **yellow** precipitate.
**EAS on benzaldehyde / acetophenone:** –CHO and –COR are **meta** directors.
:::

## Part E · Carboxylic Acids

:::formula Preparation
Oxidation of 1° alcohols and aldehydes; **alkylbenzenes + alkaline $\ce{KMnO4}$ → benzoic acid** (whatever the chain length, provided there is a benzylic H); hydrolysis of nitriles and amides; **Grignard + $\ce{CO2}$ (dry ice)**, then $\ce{H3O+}$ → RCOOH; hydrolysis of acyl halides, anhydrides and esters.
:::

:::formula Acidity
The carboxylate ion has two **equivalent** resonance structures, so carboxylic acids are much more acidic than phenols and alcohols. They liberate $\ce{CO2}$ from $\ce{NaHCO3}$ (this is the test).
- EWG raise acidity: $\ce{CF3COOH} > \ce{CCl3COOH} > \ce{CHCl2COOH} > \ce{ClCH2COOH} > \ce{HCOOH} > \ce{CH3COOH}$.
- α-substituent: F > Cl > Br > I. The effect falls off with distance: 2-chlorobutanoic > 3-chloro > 4-chloro.
- p$K_a$ values: HCOOH 3.75 · $\ce{C6H5COOH}$ 4.19 · $\ce{CH3COOH}$ 4.76. *p*-Nitrobenzoic acid is stronger than benzoic acid; *p*-methoxybenzoic acid is weaker.
:::

:::rmap Reaction map · ethanoic acid $\ce{CH3COOH}$
$\ce{NaHCO3}$ | $\ce{CH3COONa + CO2}$ (brisk effervescence)
$\ce{PCl5}$ / $\ce{PCl3}$ / $\ce{SOCl2}$ | $\ce{CH3COCl}$ (acetyl chloride)
$\ce{P2O5}$ or heat | acetic anhydride
ROH / $\ce{H+}$ | ester
$\ce{NH3}$, then heat | ammonium acetate → acetamide
$\ce{LiAlH4}$ (not $\ce{NaBH4}$) | ethanol
NaOH + CaO (soda lime), heat | methane (decarboxylation)
**HVZ:** $\ce{Cl2}$ / red P, then $\ce{H2O}$ | chloroacetic acid (α-halogenation)
:::

## Distinguishing tests (very high frequency)

| Pair | Test that separates them |
|---|---|
| aldehyde vs ketone | Tollens' (silver mirror), Fehling's (red precipitate, aliphatic aldehydes only) |
| benzaldehyde vs ethanal | Fehling's (ethanal +) and iodoform (ethanal +) |
| methyl ketone vs other ketones (pentan-2-one vs pentan-3-one) | iodoform |
| phenol vs alcohol | neutral $\ce{FeCl3}$ (violet); bromine water (white precipitate); NaOH solubility |
| phenol vs carboxylic acid | $\ce{NaHCO3}$ (acid effervesces) |
| 1°/2°/3° alcohols | Lucas test |
| ethanol vs methanol | iodoform (ethanol +) |

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- **Fehling's** is negative for aromatic aldehydes; Tollens' is positive for all aldehydes.
- Iodoform is positive for **ethanol** and **propan-2-ol** (they are oxidised to the methyl carbonyl in situ), but negative for methanol and propan-1-ol.
- Cannizzaro needs **no α-H** and **concentrated** alkali. Aldol needs α-H and **dilute** alkali.
- $\ce{NaBH4}$ doesn't reduce –COOH or esters.
- Phenol does **not** react with $\ce{NaHCO3}$, but picric acid does.
- In Williamson synthesis, never use a 3° alkyl halide.
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. Multi-step conversions (A → B → C) with named reagents; identify an intermediate or the final product.
2. Distinguishing tests (the table above).
3. Acidity orders (substituted phenols and carboxylic acids).
4. Aldol/Cannizzaro/haloform product prediction, including cross reactions.
5. Grignard products and the number of moles consumed.
6. Ether cleavage with HI; Williamson reagent choice.
7. Mechanism steps (alcohol dehydration, nucleophilic addition).

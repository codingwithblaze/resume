## Important concepts

- **Modern periodic law (Moseley):** properties are periodic functions of **atomic number**. The long form has 18 groups and 7 periods, divided into s, p, d and f blocks by the subshell being filled.
- **Elements with Z > 100** get IUPAC temporary names from digit roots: 0 nil, 1 un, 2 bi, 3 tri, 4 quad, 5 pent, 6 hex, 7 sept, 8 oct, 9 enn, plus "-ium". So 119 = ununennium (Uue), 120 = unbinilium (Ubn).
- **Periodic trends** come from effective nuclear charge and shell number: radius, ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity, valence, metallic character, nature of oxides.

## Trends at a glance

| Property | Across a period (→) | Down a group (↓) | Key exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | decreases | increases | noble gases (van der Waals radii) look large; Ga < Al (d-contraction) |
| Ionisation enthalpy | increases | decreases | **Be > B**, **N > O** (also Mg > Al, P > S); group 13 order **B > Tl > Ga > Al > In** |
| Electron gain enthalpy (more negative) | increases | decreases | **Cl more negative than F**; S more negative than O; noble gases, Be, Mg, N ≈ positive |
| Electronegativity | increases | decreases | F highest (4.0 Pauling) |
| Metallic character | decreases | increases | — |
| Oxide nature | basic → amphoteric → acidic | more basic | amphoteric: BeO, $\ce{Al2O3}$, ZnO, $\ce{Ga2O3}$, $\ce{SnO2}$, PbO₂… |

:::important Ionic radii rules
- Cation < parent atom < anion.
- **Isoelectronic species:** radius falls as $Z$ rises. $\ce{N^{3-}} > \ce{O^{2-}} > \ce{F-} > \ce{Na+} > \ce{Mg^{2+}} > \ce{Al^{3+}}$ (all 10 electrons).
- Down a group, ions of the same charge get larger.
:::

:::important Ionisation enthalpy essentials
- $IE_1 < IE_2 < IE_3 < \dots$. A **huge jump** means you've reached a noble-gas core, which tells you the group (e.g. a big jump after $IE_2$ means group 2).
- Half-filled (N: $2p^3$) and fully filled (Be: $2s^2$) subshells are extra stable, which causes the period-2 exceptions.
- Group 13 anomaly: Ga's poor 3d shielding and Tl's poor 4f/5d shielding raise their IE values.
:::

:::formula Oxides across period 3
$\ce{Na2O}$ (strongly basic) · MgO (basic) · $\ce{Al2O3}$ (amphoteric) · $\ce{SiO2}$ (weakly acidic) · $\ce{P4O10}$ (acidic) · $\ce{SO3}$ (strongly acidic) · $\ce{Cl2O7}$ (most acidic).
For the same element, a higher oxidation state gives a more acidic oxide ($\ce{Mn2O7}$ acidic, MnO basic).
:::

## Memory tricks

:::shortcut Exceptions sentence
"**Be**fore **B**oron, **N**itrogen beats **O**xygen, and **Cl**ever chlorine grabs electrons better than fluorine." That covers the three most-asked exceptions: $IE$ (Be > B, N > O) and $\Delta_{eg}H$ (Cl > F).
:::

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Electron gain enthalpy is **negative** when energy is released. "Highest electron affinity" means most negative $\Delta_{eg}H$: Cl, not F.
- The smallest atom of period 2 is F, but **He is the smallest atom overall**.
- Radii of noble gases can't be compared with covalent radii.
- $IE_2$ of Na is exceptionally high (it would break the neon core).
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. Ordering IE / EGE / radius / EN for 3–4 elements (with the exceptions above).
2. Isoelectronic ion radii.
3. Identifying group/period/block from $Z$ or configuration.
4. IUPAC names for $Z > 100$.
5. Acidic/basic/amphoteric oxides.
6. Successive IE data → group identification.
:::

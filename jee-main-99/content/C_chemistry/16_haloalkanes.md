## Important concepts

- **C–X bond:** polar ($\ce{C^{\delta+}-X^{\delta-}}$). Bond length C–F < C–Cl < C–Br < C–I; bond strength runs the other way, so **reactivity** is R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F.
- **Nucleophilic substitution** ($\ce{S_N1}$/$\ce{S_N2}$) and **β-elimination** compete. The substrate, nucleophile/base, solvent and temperature decide which wins.
- **Haloarenes** are far less reactive towards nucleophiles: resonance gives C–X partial double-bond character, the carbon is sp², the phenyl cation is unstable, and electron-rich π clouds repel the nucleophile.

## Preparation

:::formula From alcohols, alkenes and halogen exchange
| Route | Reagent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ROH → RCl | HCl + anhydrous $\ce{ZnCl2}$; $\ce{PCl3}$; $\ce{PCl5}$; **$\ce{SOCl2}$** | $\ce{SOCl2}$ is best: the byproducts $\ce{SO2}$ and HCl are gases |
| ROH → RBr / RI | $\ce{PBr3}$ (red P + $\ce{Br2}$); red P + $\ce{I2}$ | — |
| Alkene → RX | HX (Markovnikov) | peroxide effect with HBr only |
| **Finkelstein** | RCl/RBr + NaI in dry acetone → RI | NaCl/NaBr precipitate, which drives the reaction |
| **Swarts** | RCl/RBr + AgF, $\ce{Hg2F2}$, $\ce{CoF2}$ or $\ce{SbF3}$ → RF | route to alkyl fluorides |
| Haloarenes | $\ce{ArH + Cl2}$ ($\ce{FeCl3}$) | EAS |
| **Sandmeyer** | $\ce{ArN2+}$ + CuCl/HCl, CuBr/HBr, CuCN/KCN | → ArCl, ArBr, ArCN |
| **Gattermann** | $\ce{ArN2+}$ + Cu powder/HX | → ArX |
| Aryl iodide | $\ce{ArN2+}$ + KI | no Cu(I) needed |
:::

**Physical properties:** boiling point rises with halogen mass (RI > RBr > RCl > RF) and chain length, and **falls with branching**. The *para*-dihalobenzene has a higher melting point than the ortho or meta isomer (symmetric packing).

## Nucleophilic substitution: S_N1 vs S_N2

| | $\ce{S_N2}$ | $\ce{S_N1}$ |
|---|---|---|
| Steps / rate law | one step; $r = k[\ce{RX}][\ce{Nu-}]$ | two steps via a carbocation; $r = k[\ce{RX}]$ |
| Substrate reactivity | $\ce{CH3X}$ > 1° > 2° > 3° (steric hindrance) | 3° > 2° > 1° > $\ce{CH3X}$ (carbocation stability) |
| Stereochemistry | **inversion** (Walden) | **racemisation** (with some inversion) |
| Favoured by | strong nucleophile, polar aprotic solvent | weak nucleophile, polar protic solvent |
| Special | — | allylic and benzylic halides react fast (resonance-stabilised cations) |

:::rmap Reaction map · haloalkane R–X (1°, e.g. $\ce{C2H5Br}$)
aq. KOH / NaOH | ROH (alcohol)
alc. KOH, heat | alkene (β-elimination, **Saytzeff**)
NaOR′ (Williamson) | R–O–R′ (ether)
**KCN** (ionic; attacks via C) | R–C≡N (nitrile), major
**AgCN** (covalent; attacks via N) | R–N≡C (isocyanide), major
**KNO₂** | R–O–N=O (alkyl nitrite)
**AgNO₂** | R–NO₂ (nitroalkane)
$\ce{NH3}$ (excess) | $\ce{RNH2}$ (+ higher amines)
$\ce{LiAlH4}$ | RH
Mg, dry ether | RMgX (Grignard reagent)
Na, dry ether (Wurtz) | R–R
:::

:::important Grignard reagents
$\ce{RMgX}$ is made in **dry ether**. It reacts with any compound that has an acidic H (water, alcohols, amines, terminal alkynes) to give the alkane: $\ce{CH3MgBr + H2O -> CH4 + Mg(OH)Br}$. That's why every trace of moisture must be excluded. Reactions with carbonyls are covered in Unit 17.
:::

:::formula Wurtz family
**Wurtz:** $\ce{2RX + 2Na -> R-R}$. **Wurtz–Fittig:** $\ce{ArX + RX + 2Na -> Ar-R}$ (e.g. toluene from chlorobenzene + chloromethane). **Fittig:** $\ce{2ArX + 2Na -> Ar-Ar}$ (biphenyl).
:::

## Haloarenes: substitution only when activated

- Chlorobenzene → phenol needs **623 K, 300 atm** NaOH (Dow process).
- −NO₂ groups at **ortho/para** positions activate the ring: *p*-nitrochlorobenzene reacts with NaOH at 443 K; the 2,4-dinitro compound at 368 K; the 2,4,6-trinitro compound with warm water.
- **EAS** on haloarenes: ortho/para-directing but deactivating (halogenation, nitration, sulphonation, Friedel–Crafts).

## Polyhalogen compounds: environmental effects (syllabus)

| Compound | Uses | Environmental / health notes (NCERT) |
|---|---|---|
| $\ce{CH2Cl2}$ | solvent, paint remover | affects the central nervous system |
| $\ce{CHCl3}$ | solvent (formerly an anaesthetic) | oxidised by air and light to poisonous **phosgene** ($\ce{COCl2}$), so it's stored in dark bottles filled to the brim |
| $\ce{CHI3}$ (iodoform) | antiseptic (releases $\ce{I2}$) | objectionable smell; replaced by other antiseptics |
| $\ce{CCl4}$ | solvent, refrigerant feedstock | liver damage; depletes the ozone layer |
| Freons (CFCs) | refrigerants, aerosol propellants | reach the stratosphere, release Cl radicals, deplete ozone |
| DDT | insecticide (first chlorinated organic insecticide) | not biodegradable; accumulates in fatty tissue; banned in many countries |

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- KCN → nitrile but AgCN → **isocyanide**. KNO₂ → nitrite but AgNO₂ → **nitro** compound.
- Aqueous KOH substitutes; **alcoholic** KOH eliminates.
- $\ce{S_N2}$ on a chiral centre inverts the configuration. The R/S label may or may not change, depending on priorities.
- Vinyl and aryl halides don't undergo $\ce{S_N1}$ or $\ce{S_N2}$ under normal conditions.
:::

## Amines: important concepts

- **Classification:** 1° $\ce{RNH2}$, 2° $\ce{R2NH}$, 3° $\ce{R3N}$ (and quaternary salts $\ce{R4N+X-}$). N is sp³ and pyramidal (C–N–C ≈ 108°), with a lone pair that makes amines **bases and nucleophiles**.
- **Boiling points:** 1° > 2° > 3° for isomers (N–H hydrogen bonding). Amines boil higher than alkanes but lower than alcohols of similar mass.
- **Basicity** is decided by three effects together: +I effect, **solvation** of the ammonium ion (H-bonding with water), and **steric** hindrance. Aromatic amines are much weaker bases (the lone pair is delocalised into the ring).

## Preparation

:::formula Routes to amines
| Method | Transformation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Reduction of nitro compounds | $\ce{ArNO2 ->[Fe/HCl\ or\ Sn/HCl\ or\ H2/Pd] ArNH2}$ | Fe/HCl preferred (the $\ce{FeCl2}$ formed regenerates HCl) |
| Ammonolysis of alkyl halides | $\ce{RX + NH3 -> RNH2}$ (+ 2°, 3°, quaternary) | a mixture; excess $\ce{NH3}$ favours 1° |
| Reduction of nitriles | $\ce{RCN ->[LiAlH4\ or\ H2/Ni] RCH2NH2}$ | adds **one carbon** (ascending series) |
| Reduction of amides | $\ce{RCONH2 ->[LiAlH4] RCH2NH2}$ | same number of C |
| **Gabriel phthalimide** | phthalimide + KOH → + RX → hydrolysis → $\ce{RNH2}$ | pure **1° aliphatic** amines only (**not aniline**) |
| **Hofmann bromamide degradation** | $\ce{RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH -> RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O}$ | amine has **one carbon fewer** |
:::

## Basicity (NCERT p$K_b$ values; lower p$K_b$ = stronger base)

| Amine | p$K_b$ | Amine | p$K_b$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| methanamine $\ce{CH3NH2}$ | 3.38 | ethanamine $\ce{C2H5NH2}$ | 3.29 |
| N-methylmethanamine $\ce{(CH3)2NH}$ | 3.27 | N-ethylethanamine $\ce{(C2H5)2NH}$ | 3.00 |
| N,N-dimethylmethanamine $\ce{(CH3)3N}$ | 4.22 | N,N-diethylethanamine $\ce{(C2H5)3N}$ | 3.25 |
| ammonia | 4.75 | benzenamine (aniline) | 9.38 |

:::important Basicity orders to memorise
- **Methyl series (water):** $\ce{(CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3}$.
- **Ethyl series (water):** $\ce{(C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3}$.
- **Gas phase** (inductive effect only): 3° > 2° > 1° > $\ce{NH3}$.
- **Aromatic amines:** electron-donating groups raise basicity (*p*-toluidine > aniline), electron-withdrawing groups lower it (aniline > *p*-nitroaniline). Aniline ≪ $\ce{NH3}$.
:::

## Reactions of amines

:::rmap Reaction map · aniline $\ce{C6H5NH2}$
HCl | anilinium chloride (salt)
$\ce{CH3COCl}$ or $\ce{(CH3CO)2O}$ / pyridine | **acetanilide** (–NH₂ protected; activation reduced)
$\ce{C6H5COCl}$ (benzoylation) | benzanilide
$\ce{CHCl3}$ + alc. KOH (**carbylamine**) | phenyl isocyanide (foul smell): **1° amines only**
$\ce{NaNO2}$ + HCl, 273–278 K | **benzenediazonium chloride**
$\ce{C6H5SO2Cl}$ (**Hinsberg**) | N-phenylbenzenesulphonamide (soluble in alkali, as for all 1° amines)
**bromine water** | 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white precipitate)
conc. $\ce{HNO3}$ + $\ce{H2SO4}$ (unprotected) | tarry oxidation + *p*- (51%), *m*- (47%), *o*- (2%) nitroaniline
acetylate → nitrate → hydrolyse | mainly *p*-nitroaniline
conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 453–473 K | **sulphanilic acid** (exists as a zwitterion)
$\ce{AlCl3}$ (Friedel–Crafts) | **no reaction**: aniline complexes the Lewis acid
:::

:::formula Hinsberg test (benzenesulphonyl chloride)
| Amine | Product | Solubility in KOH |
|---|---|---|
| 1° | $\ce{C6H5SO2NHR}$ | **soluble** (acidic N–H) |
| 2° | $\ce{C6H5SO2NR2}$ | insoluble |
| 3° | no reaction | — |
:::

:::important Nitrous acid
1° **aliphatic** amines + $\ce{HNO2}$ give an unstable diazonium salt, which decomposes to an alcohol + $\ce{N2}$ (quantitative $\ce{N2}$ evolution). 1° **aromatic** amines give **stable arenediazonium salts** at 273–278 K (stabilised by resonance with the ring).
:::

## Diazonium salts: importance in synthesis

:::formula Replacement of $\ce{N2}$ (Ar–N₂⁺X⁻ →)
| Reagent | Product | Name |
|---|---|---|
| CuCl/HCl | ArCl | Sandmeyer |
| CuBr/HBr | ArBr | Sandmeyer |
| CuCN/KCN | ArCN | Sandmeyer |
| Cu powder + HX | ArX | Gattermann |
| KI | ArI | — |
| $\ce{HBF4}$, then heat | ArF | Balz–Schiemann |
| $\ce{H3PO2}$ or $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ | ArH | (reductive removal of –NH₂) |
| $\ce{H2O}$, warm | ArOH | — |
| $\ce{NaNO2}$/Cu (via fluoroborate) | $\ce{ArNO2}$ | — |
:::

:::formula Coupling (N₂ retained): azo dyes
- Phenol in **alkaline** medium + $\ce{C6H5N2+}$ → *p*-hydroxyazobenzene (**orange** dye).
- Aniline in **mildly acidic** medium + $\ce{C6H5N2+}$ → *p*-aminoazobenzene (**aniline yellow**; also listed in Practical Chemistry).
- Coupling is an **electrophilic** substitution: the diazonium ion is a weak electrophile, so it needs a strongly activated ring.
:::

:::shortcut Synthesis logic with diazonium salts
To put a group on a benzene ring where direct substitution fails or gives the wrong orientation, go **via the amine**: nitrate → reduce → (use –NH₂ to direct/activate) → diazotise → replace. Classic: **1,3,5-tribromobenzene** from aniline (brominate to 2,4,6-tribromoaniline, then diazotise and remove N₂ with $\ce{H3PO2}$).
:::

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Gabriel synthesis **cannot** make aniline (aryl halides don't undergo $\ce{S_N2}$).
- Hofmann bromamide **loses one carbon**; nitrile reduction **gains one carbon**.
- The carbylamine test works for 1° amines (aliphatic and aromatic) only.
- Direct nitration of aniline gives a lot of the **meta** product, because the anilinium ion ($\ce{-NH3+}$) forms in acid.
- Order of basicity in water isn't simply 3° > 2° > 1°. Solvation and sterics matter.
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. Basicity orders (aliphatic in water/gas; substituted anilines).
2. Conversions using diazonium salts (Sandmeyer, Balz–Schiemann, $\ce{H3PO2}$, coupling).
3. Distinguishing 1°/2°/3° amines (Hinsberg, carbylamine, $\ce{HNO2}$).
4. Hofmann bromamide / Gabriel / nitrile reduction: product and carbon count.
5. EAS of aniline: bromination, nitration with and without protection, sulphanilic acid.
:::

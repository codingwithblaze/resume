## Important concepts

- **Transition elements:** partly filled d subshell in the atom or in a common ion. General configuration $(n-1)d^{1-10}ns^{1-2}$. **Zn, Cd, Hg** ($d^{10}$ in both the atom and the common ion) are not regarded as transition elements.
- **Characteristic properties:** metallic, high melting points (maximum near the middle of each series; W is highest; Zn, Cd, Hg are low, and Hg is liquid), variable oxidation states, coloured ions, paramagnetism, catalytic activity, complex formation, interstitial compounds, alloys.
- **Lanthanoid contraction:** the steady decrease in size from La to Lu, caused by the poor shielding of 4f electrons.
- **Actinoids:** all radioactive; wider range of oxidation states because 5f, 6d and 7s have comparable energies.

## Trends & NCERT facts (first transition series)

:::formula Oxidation states & stability
- Sc shows only +3; Zn only +2. **Mn shows the maximum (+7)** in the first series. Os/Ru reach +8 ($\ce{OsO4}$).
- Stable half-filled $d^5$: $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ and $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$. $\ce{Mn^{3+}}$ ($d^4$) is a strong oxidant; $\ce{Cr^{2+}}$ ($d^4$) is a strong reductant.
- $E^\circ(\ce{M^{2+}/M})$: **Cu is the only first-series metal with a positive value** (+0.34 V), so it doesn't liberate $\ce{H2}$ from dilute acids.
- Selected $E^\circ(\ce{M^{3+}/M^{2+}})$: Co +1.97, Mn +1.57, Fe +0.77, Cr −0.41 V.
- Higher oxidation states are stabilised by F and O (e.g. $\ce{MnO4-}$, $\ce{CrO4^{2-}}$, $\ce{VF5}$).
:::

:::formula Colour & magnetism
$\mu_{spin-only} = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM. Colour arises from **d–d transitions**, so $d^0$ and $d^{10}$ ions are colourless ($\ce{Sc^{3+}}$, $\ce{Ti^{4+}}$, $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$, $\ce{Cu+}$).
| Aqueous ion | Colour | Aqueous ion | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\ce{Ti^{3+}}$ ($d^1$) | purple | $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ ($d^6$) | pale green |
| $\ce{V^{3+}}$ ($d^2$) | green | $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ ($d^5$) | yellow |
| $\ce{Cr^{3+}}$ ($d^3$) | violet | $\ce{Co^{2+}}$ ($d^7$) | pink |
| $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ ($d^5$) | pale pink | $\ce{Ni^{2+}}$ ($d^8$) | green |
| $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ($d^9$) | blue | $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ ($d^{10}$) | colourless |

The intense colours of $\ce{MnO4-}$ (purple) and $\ce{Cr2O7^{2-}}$ (orange) come from **charge transfer**, not d–d transitions. Both have $d^0$ metal centres.
:::

:::important Catalysts, interstitials, alloys (NCERT)
- Catalysts: $\ce{V2O5}$ (contact process, $\ce{SO2 -> SO3}$), Fe (Haber process), Ni (hydrogenation), $\ce{TiCl4}$ + $\ce{Al(CH3)3}$ (Ziegler–Natta), $\ce{MnO2}$ ($\ce{KClO3}$ decomposition). They work through variable oxidation states and adsorption on surfaces.
- Interstitial compounds (TiC, $\ce{Mn4N}$, $\ce{Fe3H}$, $\ce{TiH2}$): hard, very high melting points, conducting, chemically inert.
- Alloys form easily because the atomic radii are similar: brass (Cu–Zn), bronze (Cu–Sn), stainless steel.
:::

## Potassium dichromate and potassium permanganate (NCERT)

:::formula K₂Cr₂O₇
**Preparation from chromite:**
$$\ce{4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3 + 7O2 -> 8Na2CrO4 + 2Fe2O3 + 8CO2}$$
$$\ce{2Na2CrO4 + 2H+ -> Na2Cr2O7 + 2Na+ + H2O} \qquad \ce{Na2Cr2O7 + 2KCl -> K2Cr2O7 + 2NaCl}$$
**pH-dependent equilibrium:** $\ce{2CrO4^{2-} (yellow) + 2H+ <=> Cr2O7^{2-} (orange) + H2O}$.
**Oxidising action (acid):** $\ce{Cr2O7^{2-} + 14H+ + 6e- -> 2Cr^{3+} + 7H2O}$ ($E^\circ = 1.33$ V). It oxidises $\ce{I-}$ → $\ce{I2}$, $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ → $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$, $\ce{H2S}$ → S, $\ce{Sn^{2+}}$ → $\ce{Sn^{4+}}$.
Structure: two $\ce{CrO4}$ tetrahedra sharing one O (Cr–O–Cr angle ~126°).
:::

:::formula KMnO₄
**Preparation:** $\ce{2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2 -> 2K2MnO4 (green) + 2H2O}$; then $\ce{3MnO4^{2-} + 4H+ -> 2MnO4- + MnO2 + 2H2O}$ (disproportionation). Commercially, manganate is oxidised electrolytically.
**Oxidising action:**
| Medium | Half-reaction | n-factor |
|---|---|---|
| acidic | $\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- -> Mn^{2+} + 4H2O}$ ($E^\circ$ 1.52 V) | 5 |
| neutral / faintly alkaline | $\ce{MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- -> MnO2 + 4OH-}$ | 3 |

Acidic: $\ce{I-}$ → $\ce{I2}$; $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ → $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$; $\ce{C2O4^{2-}}$ → $\ce{CO2}$ (at 333 K); $\ce{S^{2-}}$ → S; $\ce{SO3^{2-}}$ → $\ce{SO4^{2-}}$; $\ce{NO2-}$ → $\ce{NO3-}$.
Neutral/alkaline: $\ce{I-}$ → $\ce{IO3-}$; thiosulphate → sulphate; $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ → $\ce{MnO2}$.
$\ce{KMnO4}$ is dark purple and diamagnetic ($d^0$), with weak temperature-dependent paramagnetism. Titrations with it use **dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$**, not HCl (which it would oxidise to $\ce{Cl2}$).
:::

## Inner transition elements

:::grid2
**Lanthanoids (Ce–Lu)**

- Configuration $[\text{Xe}]4f^{1-14}5d^{0-1}6s^2$. The common oxidation state is **+3**.
- Other stable states track $f^0$, $f^7$, $f^{14}$: $\ce{Ce^{4+}}$ ($f^0$, oxidant), $\ce{Eu^{2+}}$ ($f^7$, reductant), $\ce{Yb^{2+}}$ ($f^{14}$), $\ce{Tb^{4+}}$ ($f^7$).
- **Lanthanoid contraction consequences:** Zr ≈ Hf in size (they occur together and are hard to separate); lanthanoids are hard to separate from each other; basicity of $\ce{Ln(OH)3}$ decreases from La to Lu; the 5d series has higher IE and density.
- Misch metal (~95% lanthanoids + Fe) is used in lighter flints.
+++
**Actinoids (Th–Lr)**

- Configuration $[\text{Rn}]5f^{1-14}6d^{0-1}7s^2$. All are radioactive.
- **Wider range of oxidation states** (+3 to +7; e.g. Np, Pu up to +7), because 5f, 6d and 7s have comparable energies.
- Actinoid contraction is **greater** than lanthanoid contraction (5f shields even more poorly).
- Early actinoids resemble transition metals more than the lanthanoids do.
:::

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Configurations of ions: remove 4s electrons first ($\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ = $3d^5$).
- Cr and Cu atomic anomalies: $3d^54s^1$ and $3d^{10}4s^1$.
- Colour of $\ce{KMnO4}$/$\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ is **charge transfer**, not d–d.
- $\ce{KMnO4}$ titrations: never acidify with HCl.
- Zn, Cd, Hg: d-block, but not transition elements.
:::

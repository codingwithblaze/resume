## Important concepts

- **Tetravalency & hybridisation:** sp³ (25% s, tetrahedral, 109.5°), sp² (33% s, trigonal, 120°), sp (50% s, linear, 180°). More s-character makes carbon more electronegative, so sp C–H bonds are more acidic.
- **Classification & homologous series:** functional groups; successive members differ by $\ce{-CH2-}$.
- **Isomerism:** structural (chain, position, functional, metamerism, tautomerism) and stereoisomerism (geometrical, optical).
- **Bond fission:** homolytic → free radicals; heterolytic → carbocations/carbanions.
- **Electronic effects:** inductive (permanent, through σ bonds, falls off with distance), electromeric (temporary, on demand), resonance/mesomeric (π delocalisation), hyperconjugation (σ–π delocalisation).
- **Reaction types:** substitution, addition, elimination, rearrangement.

## IUPAC nomenclature essentials

1. **Principal chain:** the longest chain containing the principal functional group (and as many multiple bonds as possible).
2. **Lowest locants:** principal group first, then multiple bonds, then substituents (lowest-set rule).
3. **Suffix priority** (highest first): carboxylic acid > sulphonic acid > anhydride > ester > acyl halide > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine. Halo, nitro and alkoxy groups are always prefixes.
4. Substituent prefixes in **alphabetical** order (di-, tri- ignored).

Examples: $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2COOH}$ = 3-hydroxybutanoic acid; $\ce{CH2=CHCHO}$ = prop-2-enal; $\ce{CH3COCH2CH2Cl}$ = 4-chlorobutan-2-one.

## Stability orders (the core of GOC)

:::formula Reactive intermediates
| Intermediate | Hybridisation / shape | Stability order | Stabilised by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbocation | sp², planar | benzyl ≈ allyl (resonance) > 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl > vinyl, aryl | +I, hyperconjugation, +R |
| Free radical | sp², planar (nearly) | benzyl ≈ allyl > 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl | hyperconjugation, resonance |
| Carbanion | sp³, pyramidal | methyl > 1° > 2° > 3° (reverse) | −I, −R (e.g. next to C=O, $\ce{NO2}$) |

Triphenylmethyl cation/radical/anion are exceptionally stable (extensive resonance).
:::

:::formula Electronic-effect rankings
**−I:** $\ce{-NO2} > \ce{-CN} > \ce{-COOH} > \ce{-F} > \ce{-Cl} > \ce{-Br} > \ce{-I} > \ce{-OH} > \ce{-OR} > \ce{-C6H5}$
**+I:** $\ce{-C(CH3)3} > \ce{-CH(CH3)2} > \ce{-CH2CH3} > \ce{-CH3}$
**+R (electron-donating by resonance):** $\ce{-O-} > \ce{-NH2} > \ce{-OH} > \ce{-OR} > \ce{-NHCOR}$, halogens (weak +R, strong −I)
**−R (electron-withdrawing):** $\ce{-NO2}$, $\ce{-CN}$, $\ce{-CHO}$, $\ce{-COR}$, $\ce{-COOH}$, $\ce{-SO3H}$
**Hyperconjugation:** the number of α-hydrogens decides it: $\ce{CH3+}$ 0 < $\ce{CH3CH2+}$ 3 < $\ce{(CH3)2CH+}$ 6 < $\ce{(CH3)3C+}$ 9.
:::

:::important Electrophiles & nucleophiles
**Electrophiles** (electron-seeking): $\ce{H+}$, $\ce{NO2+}$, $\ce{Cl+}$, carbocations, $\ce{BF3}$, $\ce{AlCl3}$, carbonyl carbon.
**Nucleophiles** (nucleus-seeking): $\ce{OH-}$, $\ce{RO-}$, $\ce{CN-}$, $\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{H2O}$, $\ce{ROH}$, $\ce{RNH2}$, carbanions.
:::

## Isomerism quick reference

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Chain | butane / 2-methylpropane |
| Position | propan-1-ol / propan-2-ol |
| Functional | ethanol / methoxymethane; propanal / propanone |
| Metamerism | diethyl ether / methyl propyl ether |
| Tautomerism | keto–enol: propanone ⇌ prop-1-en-2-ol (needs an α-H) |
| Geometrical | *cis*/*trans*-but-2-ene (needs different groups on **each** double-bond carbon) |
| Optical | butan-2-ol (one chiral C); lactic acid |

**Counting stereoisomers:** up to $2^n$ for $n$ chiral centres, fewer when **meso** forms exist (2,3-dibromobutane and tartaric acid have 3: a d/l pair + meso). Meso compounds have an internal mirror plane and are optically inactive. A racemic mixture (50 : 50) is optically inactive by external compensation.

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Halogens: −I dominates +R, so they deactivate rings, but they still direct ortho/para.
- Vinyl and aryl carbocations are **very unstable** (sp carbon, no resonance help).
- Carbanion stability order is the **reverse** of the carbocation order.
- Geometrical isomerism: but-1-ene has none (two H on C1).
- A molecule with chiral centres may still be achiral (meso).
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. Ranking carbocation/radical/carbanion stability.
2. Ranking acidity/basicity using I, R and hyperconjugation effects.
3. IUPAC names of polyfunctional compounds.
4. Counting isomers (structural, stereo); identifying chiral/meso compounds.
5. Classifying reagents (electrophile/nucleophile) and reaction types.
6. Hyperconjugation structures/α-H counting; resonance structures and their relative stability.
:::

## Alkanes

:::formula Preparation & reactions
**Preparation:** hydrogenation of alkenes/alkynes (Ni, Pt or Pd); alkyl halide + Zn/HCl; **Wurtz** $\ce{2RX + 2Na ->[dry ether] R-R + 2NaX}$ (poor for unsymmetrical alkanes: gives a mixture); **decarboxylation** $\ce{RCOONa + NaOH ->[CaO, \Delta] RH + Na2CO3}$ (one carbon fewer); **Kolbe electrolysis** $\ce{2RCOONa + 2H2O -> R-R + 2CO2 + H2 + 2NaOH}$ (R–R at the anode).
**Reactions:** free-radical **halogenation** (hν). Reactivity $\ce{F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2}$; H abstraction 3° > 2° > 1°. Combustion; controlled oxidation; isomerisation ($\ce{AlCl3}$/HCl); **aromatisation** (n-hexane → benzene over $\ce{Cr2O3}$, $\ce{V2O5}$ or $\ce{Mo2O3}$ at 773 K); pyrolysis (cracking).
:::

:::important Free-radical chlorination of methane: mechanism
**Initiation:** $\ce{Cl2 ->[h\nu] 2Cl.}$ **Propagation:** $\ce{Cl. + CH4 -> HCl + CH3.}$; $\ce{CH3. + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl.}$ **Termination:** radicals combine ($\ce{Cl. + Cl.}$, $\ce{CH3. + CH3.}$ → ethane, which explains traces of ethane in the product).
:::

**Conformations of ethane:** staggered (most stable) and eclipsed (least stable), differing by about **12.5 kJ/mol** (torsional strain). They are drawn as **Sawhorse** and **Newman** projections. Free rotation at room temperature means the conformers can't be isolated.

## Alkenes

:::formula Preparation
- Alkynes: **Lindlar's catalyst** (Pd/$\ce{CaCO3}$ poisoned with S or quinoline) → **cis**-alkene. **Na in liquid $\ce{NH3}$** → **trans**-alkene.
- **Dehydrohalogenation:** alkyl halide + alcoholic KOH → alkene (Saytzeff: more substituted alkene is major).
- **Dehalogenation** of vicinal dihalides with Zn.
- **Acidic dehydration** of alcohols: conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 443 K (3° > 2° > 1° ease).
:::

:::rmap Reaction map · propene $\ce{CH3CH=CH2}$
$\ce{H2}$ / Ni | propane
$\ce{Br2}$ in $\ce{CCl4}$ (anti addition; decolourises) | 1,2-dibromopropane
HBr (no peroxide) · **Markovnikov** | 2-bromopropane
HBr + peroxide · **anti-Markovnikov** (only HBr) | 1-bromopropane
$\ce{H2O}$ / $\ce{H+}$ · Markovnikov | propan-2-ol
cold dil. alkaline $\ce{KMnO4}$ (Baeyer's test) | propane-1,2-diol (purple → brown)
hot acidic $\ce{KMnO4}$ | ethanoic acid + $\ce{CO2}$
$\ce{O3}$ then Zn/$\ce{H2O}$ (ozonolysis) | ethanal + methanal
polymerisation | polypropene
:::

:::important Markovnikov & peroxide effect: mechanisms
- **Markovnikov:** $\ce{H+}$ adds first to give the **more stable carbocation** (2° here), and then $\ce{Br-}$ attacks it.
- **Peroxide (Kharasch) effect:** HBr + ROOR gives a free-radical chain. $\ce{Br.}$ adds first to give the more stable **radical**, so Br ends up on the less substituted carbon. It doesn't occur with HCl or HI (their propagation steps are not both exothermic).
:::

## Alkynes

:::formula Key facts
**Preparation:** $\ce{CaC2 + 2H2O -> C2H2 + Ca(OH)2}$; vicinal dihalide + alc. KOH, then $\ce{NaNH2}$.
**Acidity:** terminal alkynes are weakly acidic (sp C–H): $\ce{HC#CH + Na -> HC#CNa + 1/2 H2}$. Acidity order: ethyne > ethene > ethane.
**Additions:** $\ce{H2}$, $\ce{X2}$, HX (Markovnikov, twice → gem-dihalide).
**Hydration:** $\ce{HgSO4}$/dil. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 333 K. Ethyne → **ethanal**; propyne → **propanone** (via the enol).
**Cyclic polymerisation:** 3 $\ce{C2H2}$ → benzene (red-hot iron tube, 873 K).
:::

## Aromatic hydrocarbons

:::formula Benzene & aromaticity
All C–C bonds are equal (139 pm) because of resonance. **Hückel's rule:** planar, cyclic, fully conjugated, with $(4n + 2)$ π electrons → aromatic. Examples: benzene (6), naphthalene (10), cyclopentadienyl **anion** (6), tropylium **cation** (6), pyridine. The cyclopentadienyl cation (4) is antiaromatic; cyclooctatetraene is non-planar (tub-shaped), so non-aromatic.
**Preparation:** cyclic polymerisation of ethyne; decarboxylation of sodium benzoate (soda lime); reduction of phenol with Zn dust.
:::

:::formula Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)
Mechanism: (1) generate $\ce{E+}$ → (2) attack forms the **arenium (σ) complex** (slow, rate-determining) → (3) loss of $\ce{H+}$ restores aromaticity.
| Reaction | Reagents | Electrophile |
|---|---|---|
| Nitration | conc. $\ce{HNO3}$ + conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ | $\ce{NO2+}$ |
| Halogenation | $\ce{Cl2}$/$\ce{Br2}$ + $\ce{FeCl3}$/$\ce{AlCl3}$ (dark, cold) | $\ce{Cl+}$ ($\ce{Br+}$) |
| Sulphonation | fuming $\ce{H2SO4}$ | $\ce{SO3}$ |
| Friedel–Crafts alkylation | RX + anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$ | $\ce{R+}$ (**rearranges**; polyalkylation possible) |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | RCOCl + anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$ | $\ce{RC#O+}$ (acylium; no rearrangement) |

**Addition to benzene:** $\ce{H2}$/Ni (high $T$, $P$) → cyclohexane; $\ce{Cl2}$ + UV → benzene hexachloride (BHC, $\ce{C6H6Cl6}$).
:::

:::formula Directive influence of substituents
| Group type | Examples | Directs to | Ring activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strongly activating | $\ce{-O-}$, $\ce{-NH2}$, $\ce{-NHR}$, $\ce{-OH}$, $\ce{-OR}$ | ortho/para | activating |
| Mildly activating | $\ce{-NHCOCH3}$, $\ce{-CH3}$, alkyl, $\ce{-C6H5}$ | ortho/para | activating |
| Halogens | –F, –Cl, –Br, –I | **ortho/para** | **deactivating** (−I > +R) |
| Meta directors | $\ce{-NO2}$, $\ce{-CN}$, $\ce{-CHO}$, $\ce{-COR}$, $\ce{-COOH}$, $\ce{-SO3H}$, $\ce{-NR3+}$ | meta | deactivating |

Friedel–Crafts reactions **fail** on strongly deactivated rings (e.g. nitrobenzene) and on aniline ($\ce{-NH2}$ complexes with $\ce{AlCl3}$).
Polynuclear hydrocarbons such as 3,4-benzpyrene are **carcinogenic** (NCERT).
:::

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- The peroxide effect works **only with HBr**.
- Friedel–Crafts alkylation with n-propyl chloride gives mainly **isopropylbenzene** (the 1° carbocation rearranges to 2°).
- Lindlar gives cis; Na/liquid $\ce{NH3}$ gives trans.
- Hydration of ethyne gives an **aldehyde** (ethanal). Every other alkyne gives ketones.
- Wurtz with two different halides gives **three** alkanes.
:::

## Question patterns

:::pyq Recurring structures
1. Products of addition to unsymmetrical alkenes (Markovnikov/anti-Markovnikov), ozonolysis → identifying the parent alkene.
2. Stereochemistry of reductions (Lindlar vs Na/NH₃).
3. Wurtz/Kolbe/decarboxylation products.
4. EAS reagents, electrophiles, and directive effects in disubstituted benzenes.
5. Aromaticity identification.
6. Conformations (Newman/Sawhorse) and their relative energies.
:::

## Carbohydrates

:::formula Classification
| Class | Examples | Reducing? |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharides | glucose (aldohexose), fructose (ketohexose), ribose | yes |
| Disaccharides | **sucrose** (glucose + fructose) | **no** |
| | maltose (2 α-D-glucose), lactose (galactose + glucose) | yes |
| Polysaccharides | starch, cellulose, glycogen | no |

**Reducing sugars** have a free aldehyde/ketone group (or a free hemiacetal) and reduce Tollens'/Fehling's reagents.
:::

:::important Structure of glucose: the NCERT evidence
| Reaction | Result | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Prolonged heating with HI | n-hexane | six carbons in a **straight chain** |
| $\ce{NH2OH}$ / HCN | oxime / cyanohydrin | a **carbonyl** group |
| Bromine water | gluconic acid | the carbonyl is an **aldehyde** |
| Acetic anhydride | glucose **pentaacetate** | **five –OH** groups |
| Nitric acid | saccharic (glucaric) acid | one **1° alcohol** (–CH₂OH) |

**The open chain fails to explain** why glucose gives no Schiff's test, forms no $\ce{NaHSO3}$ adduct, and why its pentaacetate doesn't react with $\ce{NH2OH}$. Hence the **cyclic hemiacetal** (pyranose, C1–C5 ring) with α and β anomers at C1. **Mutarotation** is the interconversion of the anomers in solution.
:::

:::formula Disaccharides & polysaccharides
| Sugar | Units & linkage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrose | α-D-glucose C1 – β-D-fructose C2 | non-reducing; dextrorotatory; hydrolysis gives laevorotatory **invert sugar** |
| Maltose | α-D-glucose C1 – C4 α-D-glucose | reducing |
| Lactose | β-D-galactose C1 – C4 β-D-glucose | reducing; milk sugar |
| Starch: amylose (15–20%) | α-D-glucose, C1–C4 | water-soluble, unbranched |
| Starch: amylopectin (80–85%) | α-D-glucose, C1–C4 + **C1–C6 branches** | insoluble, branched |
| Cellulose | **β**-D-glucose, C1–C4 | straight chains; plant cell walls; not digested by humans |
| Glycogen | α-D-glucose (like amylopectin, more branched) | "animal starch": liver, muscles, brain |

Fructose forms a five-membered (**furanose**) ring (C2–C5) and is laevorotatory: D-(−)-fructose.
:::

## Proteins

:::formula Amino acids & structure
- **α-Amino acids** exist as **zwitterions** ($\ce{H3N+-CHR-COO-}$). They are amphoteric, with an isoelectric point. **Glycine** is the only common achiral amino acid. Naturally occurring amino acids have the L-configuration.
- **Essential amino acids** (must be in the diet): valine, leucine, isoleucine, arginine, lysine, threonine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, histidine.
- **Peptide bond:** –CO–NH– (amide). An $n$-peptide has $(n - 1)$ peptide bonds.

| Level | Description | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | amino-acid sequence | covalent (peptide) bonds |
| Secondary | α-helix, β-pleated sheet | hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H |
| Tertiary | overall 3D folding | H-bonds, disulphide bonds, van der Waals, electrostatic forces |
| Quaternary | arrangement of subunits (e.g. haemoglobin) | same as tertiary |

**Fibrous** proteins (keratin, myosin) vs **globular** proteins (insulin, albumins).
**Denaturation** (heat, pH change) destroys secondary and tertiary structure; the **primary structure stays intact**. Examples: coagulation of egg white, curdling of milk.
**Enzymes:** mostly globular proteins, highly specific biocatalysts (e.g. maltase, invertase, pepsin).
:::

## Vitamins

| Vitamin | Solubility | Deficiency disease |
|---|---|---|
| A | fat | xerophthalmia (hardening of the cornea), night blindness |
| B₁ (thiamine) | water | beriberi (loss of appetite, retarded growth) |
| B₂ (riboflavin) | water | cheilosis (fissuring at the corners of the mouth), digestive disorders, burning sensation of the skin |
| B₆ (pyridoxine) | water | convulsions |
| B₁₂ (cyanocobalamin) | water | **pernicious anaemia** |
| C (ascorbic acid) | water | **scurvy** (bleeding gums) |
| D | fat | **rickets** (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| E | fat | increased fragility of RBCs, muscular weakness |
| K | fat | increased blood-clotting time |

Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are stored in the liver and adipose tissue. Water-soluble ones (B group, C) must be taken regularly, **except B₁₂**, which is stored in the liver.

## Nucleic acids

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | **2-deoxy-D-ribose** | D-ribose |
| Bases | A, G, C, **T** | A, G, C, **U** |
| Structure | double helix | single strand |
| Types | — | mRNA, rRNA, tRNA |
| Function | heredity (carries genetic information) | protein synthesis |

**Nucleoside** = base + sugar. **Nucleotide** = nucleoside + phosphate. Nucleotides are joined by **phosphodiester** bonds (5′–3′). Base pairing: **A=T (2 H-bonds)**, **G≡C (3 H-bonds)**.

**Hormones** (general introduction): peptide hormones (insulin, glucagon), amino-acid derivatives (thyroxine, adrenaline), and steroids (oestrogens, progesterone, testosterone). They are chemical messengers.

## Common traps

:::trap Mistake alerts
- Sucrose is **non-reducing**; maltose and lactose are reducing.
- Cellulose has **β**-links; starch and glycogen have **α**-links.
- B₁₂ is water-soluble **but** stored in the liver.
- Denaturation leaves the primary structure intact.
- Thymine is found only in DNA; uracil only in RNA.
:::

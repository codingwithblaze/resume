# The Exam-Hall Protocol {#hall-protocol}

This protocol turns 180 minutes into a routine you have rehearsed in every mock. Every part of it is a recommendation {{tag:rec}}. Test it in at least five mocks, adjust it, and then **don't change it on exam day**.

## The first 5 minutes

:::flow
Minute 0–1 · Settle | Breathe slowly: 4 counts in, 6 out, three times. Pen in hand.
Minute 1–2 · Rough sheet | Draw three columns (P, C, M) to log question numbers you skip. Write your checkpoint times at the top (see below).
Minute 2–3 · Memory dump | Write the 3–5 formulas you tend to forget. They are now out of your head and on paper.
Minute 3–5 · Start with your first subject | Open Question 1 of your first subject. Begin Round 1.
:::

:::note Instructions time
Read the on-screen instructions calmly before the exam begins, especially the NV answer format and the question-palette colours. They should already be familiar from mocks. Follow the invigilator's instructions about when you may write on the rough sheet.
:::

## Subject order

| Option | Order | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Default** | Chemistry → Physics → Maths | most students: Chemistry is fastest, banks marks early and settles nerves |
| Strongest first | your strongest → middle → weakest | students whose confidence depends on a strong start |
| Maths first | Maths → Chemistry → Physics | Maths-strong students who want the lengthiest section while fresh |

Choose one after testing each in two mocks. Record the choice on the Command Sheet.

## The round system

:::grid3
**Round 1 · Sure marks**

Go through the whole subject once. Attempt every question where you **see the method within about 20 seconds** and can finish in ≤ 2 minutes. Mark everything else **S** on your rough sheet and move on without guilt.
+++
**Round 2 · Medium questions**

Return to the S questions of that subject. Give each **up to 3 minutes**. If you're still unsure after 3 minutes, mark it for review, note how far you got, and move on.
+++
**Round 3 · Final pass (last 20 min)**

Across all subjects: finish the questions where you already have partial progress. Attempt an MCQ only after eliminating **at least two** options (page [[answer-choice]]). Enter NV answers only when solved.
:::

## Time checkpoints (9:00 start, default order)

| Clock | Elapsed | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 9:45 | 0:45 | Chemistry Rounds 1 + 2 done; move to Physics |
| 10:45 | 1:45 | Physics Rounds 1 + 2 done; move to Maths |
| 11:40 | 2:40 | Maths Rounds 1 + 2 done; start Round 3 |
| 11:50 | 2:50 | Last 10 minutes: verification only |
| 12:00 | 3:00 | End |

:::warning Behind schedule?
At each checkpoint, move on **even if the subject isn't finished**. Mark the rest S and return in Round 3. Coaching analyses of 2026 papers suggested that spending much more than 60–70 minutes on Maths often hurt overall scores [T-ANALYSIS26]. The checkpoints exist so that no subject eats another's time.
:::

## The last 20 minutes

- Stop starting long questions. Work only on those where you already have progress.
- Re-check every **NV answer entered**: value, sign, rounding and units as the question asks.
- Check the question palette: every question you meant to answer should show as answered.

## The last 10 minutes

- **No new attempts** unless the answer is certain within a minute.
- Change an answer **only** if you find a specific error in your working, not because of a feeling.
- Make sure your final responses are saved (answered-and-marked questions count as answered; confirm the rule in the on-screen instructions).

## Panic control

:::strategy When a section feels hard
1. **Remember normalisation.** Your percentile is computed within your shift (page [[exam-facts]]). If the paper is hard for you, it is hard for everyone in that shift.
2. **Reset for 60 seconds.** Pen down; five slow breaths (4 in, 6 out); read the *last line* of the next question only.
3. **Find an easy question.** Scroll to the next question in that subject that looks short. One solved question restores momentum.
4. **Switch subjects early** if the whole section is hard. Return in Round 3.
5. **Never chase a lost question.** It is worth 4 marks. Twenty minutes on it costs the 12–16 marks you could have scored elsewhere.
:::

# Subject Scoring Strategies {#subject-scoring}

Recommended attempt and accuracy targets by level {{tag:rec}}. "Level" is your current mock score in that subject (out of 100). Aim to hit the **accuracy** target first; raise attempts only once accuracy holds for three mocks.

| Subject | Level | Attempt | Accuracy target | Where the marks come from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics | Strong (≥ 70) | 21–23 | ≥ 90% | everything; watch calculation traps in mechanics |
| Physics | Average (40–70) | 16–19 | ≥ 85% | the easy block (modern physics, semiconductors, units, EM waves, KTG), current electricity, optics formulas |
| Physics | Weak (< 40) | 12–14 | ≥ 90% | the easy block only, plus direct formula questions (page [[physics-strategy]]) |
| Chemistry | Strong (≥ 75) | 23–25 | ≥ 95% | everything; NCERT exceptions decide the last marks |
| Chemistry | Average (45–75) | 19–21 | ≥ 90% | inorganic NCERT, physical numericals, biomolecules, GOC orders |
| Chemistry | Weak (< 45) | 15–17 | ≥ 90% | NCERT inorganic facts, biomolecules, practical chemistry, one-step physical questions (page [[chem-strategy]]) |
| Maths | Strong (≥ 75) | 20–22 | ≥ 90% | two passes; verify every NV |
| Maths | Average (45–75) | 16–18 | ≥ 85% | the Must-do models; the 3-minute rule |
| Maths | Weak (< 45) | 12–14 | ≥ 90% | Must-do models only (page [[maths-strategy]]) |

:::important Section B in every subject
Section B (5 NV questions per subject) has negative marking, and a guess almost never scores. Scan it in Round 1: NV questions are often direct (a count, a determinant, a stopping potential). Solve those early; leave the rest for Round 2.
:::

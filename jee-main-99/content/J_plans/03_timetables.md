# Daily Timetables {#timetables}

Four model days. Each protects **8 hours of sleep**, includes **exercise** and real breaks, and limits screens. Change the clock times to fit school, coaching and family routines, but keep the structure: hardest work when you're freshest, revision in the evening, screens off an hour before bed {{tag:rec}}.

:::grid2
**Fixed rules for every day**

- Sleep 22:00–6:00 (or any fixed 8-hour window). Same time on weekends.
- 20–45 minutes of exercise or brisk walking (the WHO recommends an average of 60 minutes of activity a day for adolescents [LS-WHO]).
- Phone outside the study room during blocks (page [[psychology]]).
- A 10–15-minute break after every block, away from the desk.
+++
**Screen limits**

- Study videos only in planned slots, at up to 1.5× speed, with notes.
- Leisure screens: up to about 60 minutes a day, after the last block, and **not in the last hour before sleep**.
- Social media: checked in breaks only, twice a day.
- Rest day: a longer leisure slot is fine, planned in advance.
:::

## Timetable A · School day (≈ 6 h of study)

| Time | Activity | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00 | Wake, water, 5 minutes of stretching | routine |
| 6:15–7:00 | **Block 0:** formula recall (R1 of the chapter from two days ago) | revision |
| 7:00–7:30 | Breakfast, get ready | break |
| 7:45–14:15 | School (travel included) | — |
| 14:30–15:15 | Lunch, rest (a nap of 20 minutes at most) | break |
| 15:15–16:45 | **Block 1:** new learning (hardest subject of the day) | learning |
| 16:45–17:00 | Break: snack, walk around | break |
| 17:00–18:30 | **Block 2:** practice on today's chapter | practice |
| 18:30–19:15 | Exercise / outdoors | health |
| 19:15–20:00 | Dinner, family | break |
| 20:00–21:30 | **Block 3:** second subject: practice or PYQs | practice |
| 21:30–22:00 | R0 recap + Mistake Book (on paper, no screens) | revision |
| 22:00 | Sleep | sleep |

## Timetable D · Mock day (exam slot 9:00–12:00)

:::note Periods and health
Plan lighter blocks (more revision, less new learning) on days you know you'll feel low in energy, rather than skipping days. If pain or fatigue regularly disrupts study, speak to a doctor. Effective options exist, and planning around the exam dates is easier with advance advice.
:::

## Paper 1 (B.E./B.Tech.) pattern

:::fact Verified from the JEE (Main) 2026 Information Bulletin [NTA-IB26]
| | Physics | Chemistry | Mathematics | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section A: MCQs (4 options, one correct) | 20 | 20 | 20 | 60 |
| Section B: Numerical-value questions | 5 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| Questions to attempt | 25 (all compulsory) | 25 | 25 | 75 |
| Marks | 100 | 100 | 100 | **300** |

- **Mode:** Computer Based Test (CBT). **Duration:** 3 hours. In 2026 the shifts ran 9:00–12:00 and 15:00–18:00.
- **Section B has no internal choice.** Since 2025 all five numerical questions are compulsory. The old 10-given-attempt-5 format is gone.
- **Languages:** 13 (English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu).
:::

## Marking scheme

:::warning Verified: the numbers that decide your strategy [NTA-IB26]
| Response | Section A (MCQ) | Section B (Numerical) |
|---|---|---|
| Correct | **+4** | **+4** |
| Incorrect | **−1** | **−1** |
| Unanswered / *Marked for Review* without an answer | 0 | 0 |

**Negative marking applies to numerical questions too.** Older books that say "no negative marking in Section B" describe a pattern that no longer exists.
:::

:::important What −1 really means
A random guess on a 4-option MCQ has expected value $\tfrac14(+4) + \tfrac34(-1) = +0.25$ marks. That looks positive, but it's tiny, and the variance is large. If you can **eliminate two options with certainty**, the expected value rises to $\tfrac12(4) + \tfrac12(-1) = +1.5$. The honest rule, used throughout this book: **attempt when you have eliminated at least two options through real reasoning. Never attempt a pure blind guess.** On numerical questions a guess is almost always wrong, so answer only after solving.
:::

## Calculators, tables, rough work

- **No calculator of any kind.** NTA's November 2025 clarification states that the on-screen calculator mentioned in the first 2026 bulletin was a typographical error, and that calculators are not allowed in JEE Main. {{tag:verified}} [NTA-CALC], [R-CALC]
- **Numerical answers:** entered with the on-screen numeric keypad. In recent papers the Section B instructions have asked for answers rounded to the nearest integer. Read the instruction on your screen on the day. {{tag:third}}
- Rough sheets are provided at the centre and must be returned. Don't carry your own paper. {{tag:verified}} [NTA-IB26]

*What this means for you:* practise all mental arithmetic, approximation and long calculation **by hand from Day 1**. The Speed Manual (page [[speed-manual]]) has drills for this.

## How your percentile is calculated

:::fact Verified: NTA normalisation [NTA-IB26], [R-NORM]
Each shift's raw scores are converted into **NTA percentile scores** separately:

$$\text{Percentile} = \frac{100 \times \text{number of candidates in that shift with raw score} \le \text{yours}}{\text{total candidates who appeared in that shift}}$$

- The topper of every shift gets 100 percentile. Percentiles are reported to 7 decimal places.
- Percentiles are also computed **subject-wise** (Physics, Chemistry, Maths). Your total percentile is computed from your total raw score. It is **not** the average of the subject percentiles.
- If you take both sessions, **the better of your two NTA scores** is used for the merit list/rank.
:::

**Why the same marks give different percentiles in different shifts:** in a harder shift, fewer candidates score high, so fewer marks are needed to beat 99% of that shift. The 99+ Strategy chapter (page [[percentile-strategy]]) shows the historical ranges.

## Tie-breaking (for rank, when percentiles are equal)

{{tag:verified}} as per the 2026 bulletin, reported in [R-TIE]. Ties are resolved in this order:

1. Higher NTA score in **Mathematics** → 2. **Physics** → 3. **Chemistry** → 4. Lower ratio of incorrect to correct answers (all subjects) → 5. same ratio in Maths → 6. Physics → 7. Chemistry → 8. Otherwise the same rank.

*Strategy note:* Maths is the first tie-breaker, and a low wrong-to-right ratio is the next. That is two more reasons, beyond the marks themselves, to protect accuracy.

## Fees, seat pools and exam-day rules

:::grid2
**Female-only seat pool (JoSAA)** {{tag:verified}}
In JoSAA counselling, IITs and the NIT+ system institutes run a *Female-only (including supernumerary)* pool alongside the *Gender-neutral* pool. The supernumerary seats exist to raise female enrolment to at least about 20%. A female candidate is considered for **both** pools, so the ranks she needs can be meaningfully different from gender-neutral closing ranks. Check the official JoSAA past opening/closing ranks for your target branches. [JOSAA]
+++
**Application fee** {{tag:verified}} (2026 bulletin, reported [R-FEE])
For one paper at an Indian centre in 2026: **₹1,000** General (male) · **₹900** Gen-EWS/OBC-NCL (male) · **₹800** female candidates of all categories · **₹500** SC/ST/PwD/third gender. Check the 2027 bulletin for current figures.
:::

:::grid2
**Exam-day rules (from 2026 guidelines)** {{tag:verified}}
Report early: in 2026, gates closed 30 minutes before the exam started. Carry your admit card, a valid photo ID and the photographs the admit card asks for. Metallic items and jewellery are restricted, and face authentication/biometric verification happens at entry. Candidates in customary or religious attire should report earlier for frisking. **The admit card's instructions override anything written here.**
+++
**Practical planning** {{tag:rec}}
- Visit or map your centre route 1–2 days before. Plan to travel with a family member for early-morning shifts.
- Female candidates: if the exam dates may coincide with your period, plan comfort and medication with your doctor in advance, and take at least two full mocks at the same time of day to rehearse. Check the admit card for permitted items.
- Keep the exam-morning routine (page [[exam-day-plan]]) identical to your mock-day routine.
:::
